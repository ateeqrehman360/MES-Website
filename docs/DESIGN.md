---

## 1. Purpose of this document

This document defines **how the Muslim Entrepreneurs website should look, feel and behave**.

The PRD defines what the product must accomplish. This document defines the intended visual experience so implementation agents do not have to invent the site's design direction themselves.

This specification should guide:

- visual design;
- typography;
- composition;
- colour usage;
- photography;
- motion;
- 3D behaviour;
- responsive behaviour;
- navigation;
- section transitions;
- supporting pages;
- accessibility adaptations.

It is not intended to specify every pixel before implementation.

Codex is allowed to exercise visual judgement within these boundaries, provided the result remains faithful to the intended character:

> **Premium. Polished. Wow.**

---

# 2. Core design idea

The website should feel like a **premium brand experience for an ambitious university entrepreneurship society**, not a conventional student-society website.

MES is the Muslim Entrepreneurs society at **Manchester Metropolitan University**.

The design should not conceal or minimise that identity. Instead, the quality of the website should challenge expectations of what a university-society website can be.

The visual character, in priority order:

1. Premium
2. Elegant
3. Luxurious
4. Corporate
5. Energetic
6. Technological
7. Experimental
8. Youthful
9. Futuristic

The finished product must avoid feeling:

> **Vibecoded. Boring. Basic.**

The site should look intentionally art-directed.

It should not look like a collection of trendy components assembled from a UI library.

It should also not look like a generic Students' Union society-page template with MES branding applied to it.

---

# 3. Organisational design positioning

MES should be visually presented as:

> **An ambitious Muslim entrepreneurship society based at Manchester Metropolitan University.**

The website is **not** the website of:

- a CIC;
- an independent external organisation;
- a hypothetical external MES branch;
- a national organisation.

The university context is part of MES's current identity and may appear naturally throughout the website.

However, **MES remains the primary visual brand**.

MMU, Students' Union and ISoc branding should provide institutional context rather than taking ownership of the visual experience.

The desired result is not:

> “Hide that MES is a university society.”

It is:

> **“Make the university society look exceptionally serious.”**

---

# 4. Overall experience hierarchy

The website has three levels of visual intensity.

### Level 1 — Hero

**Cinematic and experimental.**

This is the site's visual signature.

It may use:

- 3D;
- unusual composition;
- oversized typography;
- scroll-controlled camera movement;
- image transitions;
- depth.

### Level 2 — Major storytelling sections

**Expressive but controlled.**

Examples:

- Vision & Mission
- Impact
- Our Story
- Featured Experiences
- Community

These can have noticeable transitions and large editorial layouts.

### Level 3 — Normal content/UI

**Quiet and polished.**

Examples:

- navigation;
- forms;
- event details;
- footer;
- informational copy.

The entire website should not compete for attention simultaneously.

Core MES colours:

| Deep Green | `#01500B` | Primary contrast / authority |

Additional neutral shades may be derived where necessary.

Potential supporting uses include:

- off-white;
- cream variants;
- near-black green;
- soft neutral borders;
- muted text colours.

Do not introduce unrelated bright accent colours.

---

# 6. Colour hierarchy

## Cream

Cream should be the site's **dominant light surface**.

It replaces the sterile pure-white look common to corporate and SaaS websites.

Use it for:

- hero background;
- major light sections;
- navigation surfaces;
- editorial layouts.

## Deep Green

Deep green is the site's strongest brand colour.

Use it for:

- dark section takeovers;
- headings;
- important buttons;
- contrast surfaces;
- footer treatment;
- impact section.

It should communicate credibility and richness.

## Gold

Gold should feel **rare**.

Do not use gold as a large background merely because it exists in the logo.

Prefer gold for:

- small rules;
- selected words;
- decorative lines;
- subtle borders;
- important numbers;
- icons;
- tiny highlights;
- premium details.

Overusing gold will make the design feel less luxurious.

## Muted Green

Muted green is supporting.

Possible uses:

- secondary backgrounds;
- tags;
- soft UI states;
- section transitions;
- decorative typography.

It should never compete with deep green.

---

# 7. MES logo

The existing MES logo includes the **Muslim Entrepreneurs wordmark as part of the identity**.

Therefore, avoid repeatedly placing:

> MES logo + separate ordinary-size “Muslim Entrepreneurs” title

next to one another.

The hero may still use enormous **MUSLIM ENTREPRENEURS** typography as a compositional graphic element, because this serves a different purpose from the logo itself.

The logo should be used prominently but with restraint.

Export the best practical source format available from Canva for web use.

Prefer a vector-quality format where possible.

---

# 8. Typography direction

Use a **two-typeface system**.

## Display typography

Use an elegant, distinctive display face for:

- major statements;
- section introductions;
- large editorial words;
- selective hero typography.

It should feel:

- sophisticated;
- slightly editorial;
- premium;
- contemporary.

Avoid overly decorative luxury fonts that compromise readability.

## UI/body typography

Use a clean modern sans-serif for:

- body copy;
- navigation;
- forms;
- statistics labels;
- buttons;
- metadata;
- event details.

It should provide a modern technological counterbalance to the display typography.

---

# 9. Typography character

Important statements should often be **large enough to become part of the composition**.

Do not rely on:

- generic centred heading;
- paragraph;
- three cards;

for every section.

Large typography may:

- extend off-screen;
- overlap imagery;
- occupy unusual grid positions;
- transition independently during scroll.

On mobile, preserve impact through layout rather than simply shrinking desktop typography to tiny sizes.

---

# 10. Shape language

Corners should be **subtly rounded**.

Avoid both extremes.

### Too sharp

Can become sterile and traditional-corporate.

### Too rounded

Can become SaaS/startup/cartoon-like.

The result should feel polished and contemporary.

Cards should not all use identical rounded rectangles.

Some imagery may appear:

- full-bleed;
- borderless;
- partially cropped;
- offset;
- layered.

---

# 11. Spacing

Use generous whitespace.

The site should not feel crowded.

Desktop sections may be tall where visual storytelling benefits from space.

Spacing should support the feeling of:

> **confidence rather than emptiness.**

Do not fill blank space merely because space exists.

---

# 12. Photography philosophy

Real MES photography is an important credibility asset.

However, photography should be used selectively rather than covering every section.

Current assets are predominantly **portrait-oriented**.

The design should embrace that.

Possible treatments:

- oversized portrait crops;
- layered portrait frames;
- photographs extending outside a conventional grid;
- offset images;
- edge-to-edge imagery;
- selective masking.

Do not force every portrait image into a horizontal 16:9 card.

---

# 13. Photography quality

Prioritise photographs that communicate:

- people;
- energy;
- scale;
- speakers;
- businesses;
- interaction;
- genuine student/community activity.

Avoid using weaker photography simply because a layout contains an empty image slot.

The site should use fewer excellent photographs rather than many mediocre ones.

Photography should help demonstrate that MES is an **active university society with real people, events and external engagement**, rather than attempting to create the appearance of a larger independent organisation.

---

# 14. Motion philosophy

Motion should feel:

> **controlled, premium and intentional.**

It should not feel playful or gamified.

The hero receives the greatest motion budget.

After the hero, use animation primarily to:

- establish hierarchy;
- reveal content;
- connect sections;
- draw attention;
- create continuity.

Avoid motion that exists only because it is technically possible.

---

# 15. Lightweight motion after the hero

Suitable techniques include:

- opacity;
- transforms;
- masks;
- clipping;
- number counters;
- image reveals;
- subtle parallax where appropriate;
- typography reveals;
- horizontal logo motion.

Avoid excessive:

- blur animation;
- large continuously animated filters;
- WebGL effects;
- multiple simultaneous scroll-linked animations.

The normal website should remain inexpensive to render.

---

# 16. Main navigation

Desktop navigation:

**About · Events · Work With Us**

The MES logo returns home.

Primary CTA:

**Work With Us**

# 17. Hero navigation state

During the cinematic introduction, navigation should appear as **floating content**.

Characteristics:

- transparent background;
- no obvious nav container;
- minimal visual weight;
- sits naturally over the hero;
- logo visible;
- menu items visible;
- no major obstruction of 3D composition.

---

# 18. Post-hero navigation state

After the cinematic sequence, navigation transitions into a more conventional sticky/floating navbar.

It may gain:

- cream/light surface;
- subtle border;
- subtle shadow where appropriate;
- stronger separation from page content.

The transition should occur smoothly rather than suddenly.

---

# 19. Mobile navigation

Use a polished **full-screen mobile menu**.

Do not squeeze all navigation items into a tiny dropdown.

The menu may use:

- large typography;
- MES colours;
- strong spacing;
- simple transitions.

Keep interaction obvious.

# 20. 3D hero — core concept

Production model:

`MES_Laptop.glb`

Dedicated display mesh:

`MES_Display`

The model also contains **MES branding on the rear of the laptop display/lid**.

The laptop is the physical gateway into the MES website.

The conceptual journey:

> **Physical laptop → MES imagery → move toward display → enter display → display becomes website.**

This transition is the site's central signature interaction.

The rear MES branding should be preserved and may become part of the art direction where the camera path exposes the lid.

---

# 21. Hero initial state — 0%

The user lands directly on the finished visual composition.

There is **no animation where the laptop flies onto the screen first**.

Initial state:

- cream/light MES background;
- laptop already large and visible;
- MES logo visible;
- large editorial **MUSLIM ENTREPRENEURS** typography integrated into the composition;
- floating navigation;
- subtle scroll cue if useful.

The laptop may obscure portions of the large typography.

This creates visual depth.

---

# 22. Initial laptop composition

The laptop should occupy roughly **half the viewport or more** on suitable desktop screens.

The exact opening camera angle should be explored.

Do not force a predetermined angle if visual testing identifies something stronger.

Likely direction:

- premium three-quarter view;
- display still clearly readable;
- strong silhouette;
- opportunity to expose the MES-branded rear surface during the sequence where visually useful.

The scene should remain extremely clean.

Do not add:

- particles;
- abstract 3D spheres;
- random geometry;
- decorative floating objects;
- desk;
- room;
- unnecessary scenery.

---

# 23. Hero display initial content

Initial screen content should begin with **MES branding**.

This gives the laptop a clear purpose before introducing event photography.

As scrolling proceeds:

> Branding → Image 1 → Image 2 → Image 3 → statement → website transition.

---

# 24. Hero image count

Use a maximum of:

**3 event photographs**

during the cinematic journey.

No hero video for V1.

The three final images remain TBD until assets are reviewed.

---

# 25. Hero image transitions

Do **not** use simple slideshow crossfades by default.

Transitions should be more experimental while remaining elegant.

Possible ideas:

- directional masking;
- reveal travelling across the display;
- layered sliding planes;
- cropping that responds to laptop movement;
- one image visually replacing another through motion.

The implementation should be judged visually rather than selected purely for technical novelty.

---

# 26. Hero scroll model

The experience must be directly controlled by scroll progress.

Requirements:

- scrolling forwards advances;
- scrolling backwards reverses;
- motion remains synchronised with scroll;
- no one-way autoplay after a trigger;
- user remains in control.

Avoid aggressive scroll-jacking.

The site should still feel like the user is scrolling a webpage.

---

# 27. Hero duration

Desktop target:

**approximately 3–4 viewport heights**

of total scroll progression.

Exact duration may be tuned based on feel.

The sequence must not feel like the user is trapped waiting for the animation to finish.

There should always be visible progress.

# 28. Hero storyboard

## 0–15% — Identity

- laptop at opening angle;
- MES logo;
- oversized MUSLIM ENTREPRENEURS typography;
- MES branding on display;
- floating navigation;
- scroll cue.

The scene is calm.

## 15–30% — Movement begins

As scrolling starts:

- opening typography begins moving/fading away;
- laptop starts changing orientation;
- camera/model positioning begins evolving;
- branding remains briefly visible;
- rear MES branding may become visible depending on the approved camera path.

Movement should feel expensive and controlled.

## 30–45% — Event image one

MES branding transitions into **Image 1**.

The laptop may move slightly through the viewport.

The screen becomes increasingly important.

## 45–60% — Event image two

Experimental image transition.

Laptop orientation continues moving toward the user.

The laptop should now feel noticeably different from its opening state.

## 60–72% — Event image three

Third and final photograph appears.

Laptop is approaching front-facing orientation.

Camera is considerably closer.

Physical hardware begins becoming secondary to screen content.

## 72–82% — Statement moment

Photography clears or transitions.

Display becomes extremely minimal.

A short statement appears.

Current conceptual copy:

> **Built by Muslims.**  
> **For Muslims with ambition.**

This text is not permanently final.

What is locked is the **existence of this quiet statement moment** before entering the site.

It creates a pause between event imagery and the main website.

## 82–100% — Screen takeover

The camera advances toward `MES_Display`.

The display increasingly fills the viewport.

The physical bezel expands past the edges of the viewport.

Eventually:

- keyboard disappears;
- chassis disappears;
- bezel disappears;
- only display content remains.

The user should no longer visually distinguish:

> “I am looking at a laptop”

from:

> “I am now inside the website.”

> 3D finishes → fade to cream → homepage begins.

Instead:

> **The laptop screen itself becomes the website canvas.**

This transition must feel continuous.

# 34. Our Story

Use a roadmap/progression concept.

Potential stages:

- 2024 — founded at MMU;

- increasing events/community;
- partnerships and collaborations;

# 35. Story layout

Avoid a standard tiny-dot vertical timeline.

Preferred feeling:

> **A visual journey through MES's development.**

Desktop may use a horizontal/progressive composition if it works naturally.

Mobile should recompose into a vertical journey.

Photography and supporting numbers may appear where genuinely useful.

Between organisational story and flagship event work, include one deliberate **human moment**.

Purpose:

Remind visitors that MES is fundamentally about students, young Muslims, entrepreneurs and community.

Possible composition:

- large people-focused event photograph;
- oversized statement;
- minimal supporting copy.

This should not become a generic “Our Community” marketing block.

# 38. Featured event treatment

Each event should feel like a **mini case study**.

Possible structure per event:

- large event title;
- strong image;

# 39. Portrait event photography

Because many assets are portrait, designs may intentionally use:

- large vertical images;
- overlapping typography;
- split compositions;
- off-centre layouts;

# 40. Partners / network

Show MES's external ecosystem after visitors have seen its work.

Groups should be distinguished accurately, such as:

- Partners

Do not imply formal partnerships where none existed.

The network should demonstrate the external engagement achieved by MES **as a university society**.

# 41. Logo marquee

Use a continuous horizontal logo movement.

It should feel:

- slow;
- clean;
- premium;
- smooth;
- unobtrusive.

No need for logos to be clickable.

Avoid:

- fast ticker movement;
- huge logos;
- colourful visual chaos;
- hover gimmicks.

The marquee exists primarily to communicate scale and social proof.

# 42. University identity and context

The university context should **not** be withheld until late in the homepage merely to make MES appear independent.

MES is a Manchester Metropolitan University society, and the site should communicate this naturally.

However, there does not necessarily need to be a large standalone **“University Connection”** section if the information is already communicated more elegantly elsewhere.

University context may instead be integrated across:

- Vision/Mission;
- About;
- organisational story;
- event context;
- footer;
- relevant partner/society areas.

Where a dedicated treatment improves the story, it may be used.

Relevant context may include:

- Manchester Metropolitan University;
- Students' Union;
- current ISoc relationship;
- collaborations with university societies.

Use only accurate, approved wording.

---

# 43. University branding treatment

MMU, Students' Union and ISoc visual identities are **supporting identities**.

MES remains the primary design system.

University/institutional logos may be used where they provide:

- context;
- credibility;
- relationship clarity.

Do not redesign the site around MMU colours or generic university visual conventions.

Do not visually imply that MES is independent of MMU where that would be inaccurate.

The desired balance is:

> **Clearly university-based. Distinctively MES.**

---

# 44. Remove external-branch messaging

Do not use copy or visual storytelling that implies MES has already grown from a university society into a separate external organisation.

In particular, reconsider language such as:

> **Born in Manchester. Built beyond campus.**

This may imply an organisational transition that has not occurred.

Alternative messaging should celebrate ambition, reach and external engagement **without misrepresenting the organisational structure**.

Potential conceptual directions include:

> **Built at MMU. Connected across Manchester.**

or:

> **From campus to community.**

These are conceptual examples only and are not final approved copy.

# 57. Mobile hero composition

Potential direction:

- MES logo high in composition;
- large MUSLIM ENTREPRENEURS typography;

# 58. Mobile hero duration

The desktop sequence may use approximately 3–4 viewport heights.

# 59. Mobile image behaviour

Use that advantage.

Laptop screen imagery should maintain useful composition without requiring extreme cropping.

# 60. Mobile 3D quality strategy

Start with real 3D.

Optimise progressively through:

- cheaper lighting;
- reduced internal pixel ratio;
- smaller textures;
- fewer expensive effects;
- simpler shadows;
- shorter animation;
- lower rendering workload.

Only introduce a static/2D fallback where real testing demonstrates that it is necessary.

Test physically on:

# 62. Loading design

Preferred behaviour:

> **No dedicated loading screen.**

Useful HTML/CSS should render immediately.

The 3D scene should initialise progressively.

The page must not show:

> “Loading 3D… 67%”

unless testing proves there is genuinely no better option.

---

# 63. Loading fallback concept

If a loader eventually becomes necessary on slower devices, the preferred backup concept is:

**MES logo with colour filling vertically as loading progress increases.**

This is a fallback idea only.

It should not be implemented unless performance testing demonstrates a need.

---

# 64. Reduced motion

Users with:

`prefers-reduced-motion`

should receive a deliberately designed simplified experience.

Do not simply disable everything and leave a broken composition.

Possible behaviour:

- static laptop composition;
- gentle fades;
- shortened transition;
- normal scrolling.

All information remains available.

---

# 65. Accessibility visual requirements

Design should support:

- clear focus states;
- keyboard navigation;
- sufficient contrast;
- readable font sizes;
- meaningful hover/focus equivalence;
- accessible menus;
- reduced motion;
- meaningful alt text.

Do not create text that only becomes legible over imagery because of a fragile blend mode.

---

# 66. Performance is part of visual design

A beautiful site that stutters on mobile fails the design brief.

Particularly avoid:

- huge textures;
- excessive blur;
- large video backgrounds;
- continuous WebGL rendering when unnecessary;
- high-cost post-processing;
- multiple competing scroll libraries;
- massive font payloads.

Visual quality must be achieved intelligently rather than by brute force.

---

# 67. 3D scene lifecycle

Once the visitor has fully transitioned out of the hero, the scene should stop consuming unnecessary GPU resources.

Implementation should be designed so the laptop scene can:

- pause;
- reduce rendering;
- unmount;
- otherwise stop significant work

once no longer needed.

Exact implementation is technical, but the visual requirement is:

> leaving the hero should make the rest of the site feel lightweight.

---

# 68. Responsive principle

Do not define responsiveness as:

> desktop layout / 2.

Each breakpoint may change:

- composition;
- image positioning;
- type scale;
- section order within a composition;
- animation distance;
- hero duration;
- navigation treatment.

The identity stays consistent while layout adapts.

---

# 69. Desktop freedom

Desktop may use:

- asymmetry;
- typography partially outside containers;
- overlapping photographs;
- wide negative space;
- unusual grid proportions;
- large image treatments.

This experimentation is encouraged where usability remains clear.

---

# 70. Tablet

Tablet should not be neglected as an awkward midpoint.

Test:

- landscape tablet;
- portrait tablet;
- smaller laptop-sized browser widths.

Particular attention:

- hero camera framing;
- typography collision;
- navbar spacing;
- event compositions.

---

# 71. UI component character

Buttons, inputs and content controls should feel:

- simple;
- premium;
- confident.

Avoid generic component-library appearance.

Primary buttons may use deep green with restrained hover treatment.

Secondary actions may use:

- text link;
- outlined treatment;
- subtle gold detail.

Avoid large gradients and neon effects.

---

# 72. Hover effects

Desktop hover effects should be subtle.

Examples:

- slight image scale;
- underline/reveal;
- small directional movement;
- border/detail change.

Avoid:

- large bouncing;
- 3D card tilting everywhere;
- cursor gimmicks;
- excessive glow.

---

# 73. Form design

If/where forms exist:

- generous spacing;
- clear labels;
- excellent focus treatment;
- obvious errors;
- subtle rounded corners;
- strong submit state.

Do not use placeholder text as the only label.

---

# 74. Visual anti-patterns

Do not produce:

- purple/blue startup gradients;
- glassmorphism everywhere;
- random glowing spheres;
- excessive noise texture;
- huge pill buttons everywhere;
- every section inside a card;
- Bento grids simply because they are fashionable;
- floating 3D icons;
- decorative dashboards;
- excessive blobs;
- generic stock imagery;
- random Arabic decoration;
- generic Students' Union template styling.

Every visual device must serve the MES brand.

---

# 75. Content anti-patterns

Do not clutter screens with excessive explanatory copy.

Where possible:

**show evidence rather than claiming credibility.**

Prefer:

- numbers;
- event imagery;
- logos;
- testimonials;
- achievements.

Over:

> “We are an innovative and dynamic organisation committed to empowering…”

unless there is genuinely useful substance behind it.

Do not use corporate language to obscure the fact that MES is a university society.

Being precise about what MES is should strengthen credibility.

---

# 76. Desktop hero acceptance criteria

The hero is successful when:

- the laptop is visible immediately;
- opening composition feels intentional;
- large typography and laptop interact visually;
- event imagery is clearly visible;
- scroll progress directly controls motion;
- reverse scrolling works;
- laptop gradually becomes front-facing;
- screen takeover feels like camera movement;
- transition into the website is seamless;
- no obvious “3D ended here” cut exists.

---

# 77. Mobile hero acceptance criteria

Mobile hero is successful when:

- page begins rendering quickly;
- laptop remains visually impressive;
- composition feels purpose-built for portrait;
- scrolling stays smooth;
- touch behaviour is natural;
- screen transition is preserved;
- device does not become unusably hot;
- animations do not noticeably hitch;
- the user is never trapped behind a long loader.

---

# 78. Homepage acceptance criteria

Homepage should not feel like stacked template sections.

A visitor should experience a clear narrative:

> **Identity → Purpose → Proof → Growth → People → Work → Network → Partnership → Community**

By the end of that journey, visitors should understand both:

- the ambition and quality of MES;
- that MES is the Muslim entrepreneurship society based at Manchester Metropolitan University.

These should reinforce rather than contradict one another.

---

# 79. Visual quality hierarchy

When forced to choose:

### Beauty vs technical complexity

**Beauty wins.**

### Mobile performance vs identical desktop effects

Decide case-by-case, with performance carrying substantial weight.

### Animation vs clarity

**Clarity wins.**

### More content vs stronger presentation

**Stronger presentation wins.**

### Trendiness vs MES identity

**MES identity wins.**

### Artificial organisational prestige vs accurate positioning

**Accuracy wins.**

The design itself should provide the prestige.

---

# 80. Design freedom for Codex

Codex may:

- adjust compositions;
- recommend better transitions;
- revise layout structure;
- alter animation paths;
- suggest alternative visual techniques;

when doing so improves the result while preserving this specification.

Codex should **not blindly implement weak ideas merely because an early concept mentioned them**.

If a better solution achieves the same product intent, use it.

Codex must not independently reposition MES as an external organisation simply because doing so appears more commercially impressive.

---

# 81. Areas intentionally left open

The following should be explored during actual visual implementation:

- exact typography families;
- exact initial laptop angle;
- exact hero text positioning;
- exact use of the rear laptop branding during the camera path;
- exact screen-image transition technique;
- final three hero photographs;
- exact final hero statement wording;
- exact Vision statement;
- exact section transition animation;
- precise spacing scale;
- exact border radii;
- exact derived neutral palette;
- final event layouts;
- exact photography crops;
- exact placement/frequency of MMU and ISoc references.

These are **design decisions**, not missing product requirements.

---

# 82. Recommended design review process

Do not build the entire site and judge it at the end.

Visually review in stages:

**Hero static composition**  
→ **Hero animation**  
→ **Hero mobile**  
→ **Vision + Impact**  
→ **Story + Featured Events**  
→ **Network + Work With Us**  
→ **Supporting pages**  
→ **Final responsive polish**

If an early stage looks weak, fix it before layering more work on top.

---

# 83. Final design instruction

> **Build a website that feels intentionally designed for Muslim Entrepreneurs at Manchester Metropolitan University, not a premium template with MES colours applied to it and not a generic student-society website. Do not conceal the university identity and do not invent an external organisational identity to make MES appear larger. Instead, make the quality of MES's actual events, community, partnerships, visual identity and digital execution provide the credibility. The 3D laptop should create the initial wow, but the quality of the typography, composition, photography, spacing and storytelling must sustain that impression once the 3D ends. Use motion with restraint, preserve the premium light MES identity, make deep green the major contrast colour, reserve gold for deliberate details, and treat mobile performance as part of the visual experience rather than an optimisation task.**
